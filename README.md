# Brain Tumor MRI Review

A curated list of 18 studies cited in a review of artificial intelligence methods for brain tumor MRI analysis. The studies are grouped by task: detection and screening, segmentation, and classification. This is a selected reading list, not a comprehensive bibliography or a repository of the papers' source code.

## Detection and screening

Image-level screening and lesion localization are distinct tasks. The task descriptions below should not be interpreted as interchangeable measures of detection performance.

1. Yan, F., et al. (2023). **An Explainable Brain Tumor Detection Framework for MRI Analysis.** *Applied Sciences*, 13(6). Image-level screening and explainability. [DOI: 10.3390/app13063438](https://doi.org/10.3390/app13063438).
2. Huang, J., et al. (2025). **YOLO-TumorNet: An innovative model for enhancing brain tumor detection performance.** *Alexandria Engineering Journal*, 119, 211-221. Object detection. [DOI: 10.1016/j.aej.2025.01.062](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aej.2025.01.062).
3. Abraham, L. A., et al. (2025). **Dilated Convolution and YOLOv8 Feature Extraction Network: An Improved Method for MRI-Based Brain Tumor Detection.** *IEEE Access*, 13, 27238-27256. Object detection. [DOI: 10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3539924](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2025.3539924).
4. Jemina, S. L., and Thanarajan, T. (2025). **An intelligent brain tumor detection model using lightweight hybrid twin attentive pyramid convolutional network.** *Scientific Reports*, 15, 40177. Image-level tumor screening/classification. [DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-23813-2](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-23813-2).
5. Yang, S., et al. (2025). **Enhanced Brain Tumor Detection Using DCGAN Augmentation and Optimized EfficientDet in IoT-Based Healthcare Industry 5.0.** *IEEE Internet of Things Journal*, 12(22), 46093-46103. Detection and data augmentation. [DOI: 10.1109/JIOT.2025.3575759](https://doi.org/10.1109/JIOT.2025.3575759).
6. Rastogi, D., et al. (2026). **Dual-stage deep learning framework for brain tumor classification and localization using multimodal MRI scans.** *Intelligence-Based Medicine*, 13, 100361. Classification followed by localization. [DOI: 10.1016/j.ibmed.2026.100361](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ibmed.2026.100361).

## Segmentation

1. Raza, R., et al. (2023). **dResU-Net: 3D deep residual U-Net based brain tumor segmentation from multimodal MRI.** *Biomedical Signal Processing and Control*, 79, 103861. Three-dimensional CNN. [DOI: 10.1016/j.bspc.2022.103861](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bspc.2022.103861).
2. Magadza, T., and Viriri, S. (2023). **Efficient nnU-Net for Brain Tumor Segmentation.** *IEEE Access*, 11, 126386-126397. Efficient U-Net variant. [DOI: 10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3329517](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3329517).
3. Su, J., et al. (2024). **Reconstruct incomplete relation for incomplete modality brain tumor segmentation.** *Neural Networks*, 180, 106657. Missing-modality segmentation. [DOI: 10.1016/j.neunet.2024.106657](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neunet.2024.106657).
4. Manthe, M., et al. (2024). **Federated brain tumor segmentation: An extensive benchmark.** *Medical Image Analysis*, 97, 103270. Federated segmentation benchmark. [DOI: 10.1016/j.media.2024.103270](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.media.2024.103270).
5. Zhu, Z., et al. (2025). **Visually Stabilized Mamba U-Shaped Network With Strong Inductive Bias for 3-D Brain Tumor Segmentation.** *IEEE Transactions on Instrumentation and Measurement*, 74, 1-11. Mamba-based segmentation. [DOI: 10.1109/TIM.2025.3551581](https://doi.org/10.1109/TIM.2025.3551581).
6. Zou, Y., et al. (2026). **MGML: A plug-and-play meta-guided multi-modal learning framework for incomplete multimodal brain tumor segmentation.** *Neural Networks*, 197, 108525. Missing-modality fusion. [DOI: 10.1016/j.neunet.2025.108525](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neunet.2025.108525).

## Classification

1. Aamir, M., et al. (2022). **A deep learning approach for brain tumor classification using MRI images.** *Computers and Electrical Engineering*, 101, 108105. Deep learning classification. [DOI: 10.1016/j.compeleceng.2022.108105](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compeleceng.2022.108105).
2. Kibriya, H., et al. (2023). **A Novel Approach for Brain Tumor Classification Using an Ensemble of Deep and Hand-Crafted Features.** *Sensors*, 23(10). Deep and handcrafted feature fusion. [DOI: 10.3390/s23104693](https://doi.org/10.3390/s23104693).
3. Ferdous, G. J., et al. (2023). **LCDEiT: A Linear Complexity Data-Efficient Image Transformer for MRI Brain Tumor Classification.** *IEEE Access*, 11, 20337-20350. Vision Transformer. [DOI: 10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3244228](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3244228).
4. Wang, J., et al. (2024). **RanMerFormer: Randomized vision transformer with token merging for brain tumor classification.** *Neurocomputing*, 573, 127216. Token-merging Transformer. [DOI: 10.1016/j.neucom.2023.127216](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neucom.2023.127216).
5. Agrawal, T., et al. (2024). **A comparative study of brain tumor classification on unbalanced dataset using deep neural networks.** *Biomedical Signal Processing and Control*, 94, 106256. Classification under class imbalance. [DOI: 10.1016/j.bspc.2024.106256](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bspc.2024.106256).
6. Sadr, H., et al. (2025). **Enhancing brain tumor classification in MRI images: A deep learning-based approach for accurate diagnosis.** *Image and Vision Computing*, 159, 105555. CNN-based classification. [DOI: 10.1016/j.imavis.2025.105555](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.imavis.2025.105555).
